"בניטה" הסוחף והתזזיתי של ברלינר הוא מחווה לאישה קרובה שעזבה וליצירה שלא הספיקה להשלים.

## Synopsis (EN) *(agent-authored translation; Docaviv doesn't publish EN descriptions)*
"How well did I really know her?" That question, posed by the celebrated documentarian Alan Berliner ("Nobody's Business," "The Sweetest Sound") at the opening of his new film, is the engine that propels him to dive into the rich archive left by Benita Raphan, a New York visual artist and filmmaker who was, above all, a longtime friend. Longing and a detective's drive push him into a Sisyphean, obsessive task: rummaging through dozens of hard drives, countless journals, experimental films and stills, through which he tells, in cut-and-paste form, the story of her life and death. Berliner lets us not only hear about Benita from family and friends but also experience reality through her voice and her eyes, via the images she chose to leave behind — a turbulent artist who lived on the edge, with the joy of making and an emotional void that could not be filled. Berliner's sweeping, hectic "Benita" is a tribute to a close woman who left, and to the creative life she did not have time to complete.
