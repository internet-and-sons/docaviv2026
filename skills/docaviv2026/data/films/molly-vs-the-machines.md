When you say goodnight to your children, turn off the light and lock the front door, you may feel you've done everything you can to protect them. But if a child has a smartphone in their hand — a window open to the outside world — the decisions about what they will see, what will accompany them and what will penetrate their soul are no longer in their hands or yours, but in the hands of Silicon Valley. This chilling realization is sharpened by the tragic story of 14-year-old Molly Russell and her family's struggle for children's safety online. By reconstructing the inquest into the circumstances of Molly's death, the film makes a brilliant choice to give artificial intelligence too an opportunity to speak for itself, to expose how the algorithm turns us all into a database.
